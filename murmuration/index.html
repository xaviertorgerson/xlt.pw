How to play

Tools (bar at the bottom)

Hunter
Click or tap and a falcon (or shark) dives at that spot. Press and hold and it chases where you point until you let go.
Startle
Click near the flock to send a panic wave from that spot.
Lure
Press and hold: the flock is drawn toward where you point.
Add hunter
Click to release a wild hawk, falcon, shark or tuna there.
Inspect
Click one bird to see its size, pace, boldness and sociability, then ride along with it.

Moving around

The same on every device: drag looks, tap acts, hold grabs.

Mouse
Drag to look around; the wheel zooms. Click for the tool’s action. Hold the button still for a moment to take hold of the hunter or lure, or use the right button (or Shift) to take hold at once.
Trackpad
The same, and a two-finger swipe looks around and a pinch zooms.
Touch
Drag to look around; pinch to zoom. Tap for the tool’s action. Press and hold to take hold. Tap with three fingers for the next view.

What you are seeing

Each bird watches only its seven nearest neighbours: it matches their heading and pace, keeps its space, speeds up when it falls behind and eases off out in front. It also sees where the bulk of the flock is, and strays back over home if it wanders past the edge, the way starlings swirl above their roost. Turns and startles pass from bird to bird, which is why waves sweep through the swarm. No two birds are quite alike: some are bigger and quicker, some bolder, some follow their neighbours more closely.

Murmurations happen at dusk in winter, when starlings gather over a roost; the display is thought to protect them from falcons and hawks, which struggle to single out one bird in a shifting mass. Fish schools do the same under attack from sharks and tuna, sometimes balling up tightly.
